# Search and selection note

Version 1.0.0 · 26 October 2026

## Purpose and routes

This is a curated comparison used to position an empirical study of recorded governing utilisation. It is not a systematic review, a prevalence meta-analysis or a census of global practice. Seven original manuscript comparators were supplemented through four Exa search passes on 26 October 2026: Australia/New Zealand; United States academic studies; United States industry and professional bodies; and global utilisation audits. Citation tracing and direct publisher, university-repository and report checks followed. Bibliographic details and source limits were reconciled when building this package.

The working search note records `sources_reviewed: 378`. That is a search-process count, not a reproducible count of unique deduplicated records or full texts screened. No PRISMA screening count, complete search-string archive or exhaustive coverage is claimed. The structured table states the access/verification boundary for each included entry. The original working note remains an internal record; it is superseded by S1 for the selected table content.

## Inclusion and organisation

Retain studies that quantify member utilisation or conditional reserve; compare provided and redesigned reinforcement; or explain how redesign, structural-system choice, material quantity, cost and carbon relate to interpretation of a utilisation benchmark. Direct governing-utilisation evidence is distinguished from related outcomes. The 22 entries form three panels: utilisation/reserve/reinforcement (6); redesign/system/optimisation comparisons (10); and multi-project quantity/carbon datasets (6). Panel membership describes the principal role of an entry and is not a quality ranking.

## Main-table selection

The eight main entries are S01, S02, S04, S06, S07, S08, S17 and S20. The selection retains the closest observed steel utilisation comparators; concrete reinforcement and conditional reserve; fixed-constraint redesign and generated-frame appraisal; and single-firm versus multi-firm project-level quantity/carbon evidence. This locates the remaining gap in measure and population coverage, rather than project count alone. Geography, sample size or a large saving percentage alone is insufficient for selection.

Forster (S05) remains explicitly acknowledged in the main prose because its concrete-utilisation evidence is closely related. It is outside the compact numerical table because only a one-page abstract is available and the sample counts and appraisal details are missing. Miller (S09) and Helal (S11) retain Australian context in a short synthesis sentence. All other entries remain accessible in S1; their placement does not imply that they are invalid.

## Exclusions and related sources

- Dunant et al. (2021), DOI 10.1016/j.istruc.2021.04.033, reuses 19 designs from Dunant et al. (2018); it is not another independent empirical row.
- Webster/SEI (2020) and later professional summaries repeat Moynihan and Allwood (2014). Gholam's discussion repeats the Dunant sample. They are not separate US or other utilisation audits.
- Earlier Thornton Tomasetti reports on more than 600 structures belong to the tracking programme later analysed by Fang et al. (2026). They are not added as another independent population.
- Helal et al.'s IOP inventory-method comparison, DOI 10.1088/1755-1315/588/3/032028, measures inventory-method sensitivity rather than member reserve.
- Concrete-mixture strength overdesign, seismic overstrength factors, reliability calibration and comparisons between design-code sizing rules measure different constructs and are outside this comparison's utilisation scope.
- Practitioner surveys about target utilisation and reasons for reserve are contextual evidence, not observed member-utilisation distributions.
- Policy pages, EPDs, design guidance, national stock estimates and sources without a retrievable relevant result were not added as empirical study rows.

## Verification and overlap limits

The qualitative Sadek entry excludes the secondary 30%, 60% and 240% figures because those figures were not verified in the primary full text. Forster has no inferred sample count. The unverified office percentiles from the CLF companion report are excluded. The CLF 292-project dataset paper and companion report are one entry; the California sample is part of the same programme. Other industry contributions may overlap CLF and SE 2050. Project, model, member and submission counts are kept distinct and are never summed.

No Australian or United States multi-project audit of routine governing member demand-to-capacity ratios was identified in this search. This describes the search outcome; it does not prove that none exists. The present manuscript's gap statement remains bounded to the reviewed evidence and its sampled platform population.
